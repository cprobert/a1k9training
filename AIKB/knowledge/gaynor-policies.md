# Gaynor's policies — the site's canonical knowledge

Every policy statement below is Gaynor Probert's own answer, given in a
structured interview on 2026-09-14, to the open questions four years of
contact-form enquiries had raised (the 2026-09-13 enquiry analysis, "What
People Ask Gaynor," is what generated the question list). The verbatim
transcript is `planning/sources/2026-09-14-gaynor-interview.md`; `[seg NN]`
below is that file's segment number.

## How to use this file

- **Statements here are canonical.** A page may say *less* than a statement
  below — the facts block on a course page doesn't need every nuance — but it
  may never say something *different*. If a page and this file disagree, the
  page is wrong.
- **A change of policy is a new dated entry here first, then the site.** Don't
  edit a template to reflect a new answer from Gaynor without recording that
  answer here — otherwise the next person to write a page has no source to
  check it against.
- **The machine-readable subset lives in `src/config/business.js`** (the
  `courses` and `sessions` blocks) and must match this file exactly on the
  figures both carry — price, deposit, class size, join-by rule. If they
  diverge, `business.js` is the bug, not this file.
- Entries are grouped by subject, not by transcript order. Where a page
  statement predates the interview and this file corrects it, that is noted
  under **Replaces**.

## Every course

- **Rolling six-week cycle, no gap.** A course runs for six weeks; the next
  course in that timetable starts the following week — never a gap.
  "So when one course ends... The next week another course starts. It's
  every six weeks they sit for six weeks. At the end of the six weeks, the
  following week will be a new course." [seg 58–59]

- **Weekends only.** No weekday, daytime or evening classes.
  "We can only [do weekends]." [seg 129]

- **Junior & Bronze on Sunday afternoons; Silver & Gold on Saturday
  afternoons.** Confirmed directly.
  "Yes." [seg 70], confirming Courtenay's summary at [seg 69].

- **Platinum is not open to direct entry** — *superseded 2026-09-18, see
  Skipping levels below: direct entry is allowed at the right obedience
  level.* It is the highest, all-off-lead,
  distance-control stage; a class only runs when enough Gold graduates want
  to move up, and it's arranged with the group at Gold graduation.
  "People don't come in on a Platinum. They have to work their way up to
  that. Because it's the highest stage of training." [seg 62]
  "I've got to have enough people moving up from gold to make a platinum...
  it entirely depends on how many people want to do that course." [seg 65–66]
  "That's fine." [seg 68], confirming "Gold members will discuss that
  directly with Gaynor at the graduation of gold."

- **Class size: about ten dogs, never more than twelve.** Applies to every
  course, not only puppy socialisation.
  "Every course is about ten dogs max." [seg 175]
  "If I'm pushed... but no more than twelve. That's it. That's max then."
  [seg 180]

- **Joining late: week two only, if there's space; after that, the next
  course. No make-up classes for a missed week.** You only get the weeks
  you paid for.
  "They can join on the second week. After that, no." [seg 75]
  "No. See, because they've paid for their spot in that course... they only
  get the weeks that they pay for." [seg 161]

- **Booking steps.** Website form → the enquiry arrives as an email →
  Gaynor replies with the course details → the client confirms → deposit by
  bank transfer → Gaynor confirms receipt and dates → a WhatsApp group
  starts nearer the course.
  "I try and send them to the website to fill in the form. That comes
  through as an email. I reply to the email with all the details of the
  course, and then they just come back and say yes, I want to join in, and
  then I reply to that and tell them I've just received your deposit." [seg
  79] "To ask them to watch out for the WhatsApp, which will start closer to
  the time of the course date start." [seg 80]

- **Course booking steps 2 and 4, in Gaynor's wording** (2026-09-18,
  WhatsApp feedback on the Bronze page, relayed by Courtenay). Step 2:
  "Gaynor replies to your email with all the information you will need."
  Step 4 (every course but Puppy): "The balance to be paid on the first
  week of attending." These replaced the reply-channel advice in step 2 and
  "balance in cash, no instalments, no multi-dog discount" plus the WhatsApp
  note in step 4. The cash/instalment/discount policy below still stands,
  but Gaynor would "rather leave it out" of the site (2026-09-18): don't
  restate it on course pages or in llms.txt. Still on the site, pending her
  say: the two-dogs FAQ's "no discount" answer (its question asks). The
  Puppy booking strip's "in cash" / "no multi-dog discount" came out 2026-09-18.

- **Reply channels: email or text preferred over phoning.** If you haven't
  heard back after a few days, ring or text again — some enquiries reach
  A1K9 (the protection-dog business) first and take longer to be forwarded.
  "Email, text. I'm happy with all of that. If they haven't heard, they can
  ring me or text me again." [seg 102] "What happens sometimes is they think
  they've got in touch with me and they've got in touch with A1K9... it does
  come to me eventually, but it just takes a bit longer. Charles forwards
  them to me." [seg 103–104]

- **Deposit £50, non-refundable, by bank transfer — not for Puppy
  Socialisation.** The £50 figure itself is a pre-existing site figure, not
  confirmed in this interview (see Open Questions); the no-deposit-for-Puppy
  rule is.
  "They don't pay a deposit, puppy." [seg 13] "They just book a place and
  they come and they pay." [seg 15]

- **Balance paid in cash on week one. No instalments. No multi-dog
  discount** (price is per dog).
  "It's cash. It's cash." [seg 153]
  "Do I accept instalments?... I never take weekly money. I learned not to
  do that a long time ago." [seg 155–158]
  "I don't offer a discount. It's the same price for both dogs." [seg 121]

- **Two dogs from one home: allowed but not recommended.** They focus on
  each other rather than the handler and learn less; each dog needs its own
  handler; separate courses are better, especially for the obedience
  levels.
  "Two dogs from the same house will all be more interested in each other
  than they are in you. Then they don't learn because they're always
  looking for each other. So it is better to bring one dog at a time into
  class." [seg 93] "They have and they do, but it's not what I recommend.
  It's better to come in a different course." [seg 123]

- **Couples welcome.** For the dog's benefit, one person trains the dog
  through the course while the other watches and helps out at home.
  "I'm okay with both a couple coming. Very often it is a couple. But for
  the dog's benefit, it is better for one person to do the training with the
  dog. The other person is looking and learning... they help out at home
  when they're taking the dog for a walk." [seg 113]

- **Children as handlers.** From about twelve years old, a child can handle
  the dog in obedience classes; at puppy class everyone, including young
  children, joins in.
  "When we're in puppy class, everybody joins in, including very young
  children. But once they've moved up, and the dog's a little bit older,
  about twelve-year-olds can do the handling." [seg 131]

- **Catchment area.** Mainly Swansea, Neath, Port Talbot, Ammanford, down
  towards Carmarthen and Cross Hands; some clients also travel from
  Caerphilly and Cardiff — the distance judgement is theirs to make.
  "My main catchment area is Swansea, then Neath, Port Talbot... Ammanford...
  going down towards Carmarthen, Cross Hands." [seg 85–87]
  "I have people come from Caerphilly, from Cardiff. Not many, but there are
  people that come from far away... it just depends on the individual
  whether they feel it's worth doing." [seg 89]

- **Rescue and recently rehomed dogs: welcome, start whenever.** An owner
  may optionally wait about two weeks to settle the dog in first — not
  required.
  "They're welcome to come and they can start whenever they want. They
  don't have to wait. They can wait for the dog to settle a bit if they
  want to." [seg 72] "Say two weeks to get to know the dog a little bit
  before they come into class." [seg 73]

- **Restarting after a gap of months or years: normally Bronze.** Can start
  higher if the previous standard has been kept up. (2026-09-18: Gaynor
  asked for the FAQ answer to end there, without the "ring for a chat" or
  the one-to-one-after-Platinum lines.)
  "Certainly they come back in. Normally they come back in on bronze. If the
  person thinks they're at a higher level, they can come in at a higher
  level as well." [seg 182]

- **Aggressive or fearful dog wanting a group course.** A behaviour
  consultation comes first to assess suitability. If the aggression is
  controllable, the dog can attend but stays on lead throughout — it does
  not join the off-lead play, watches from outside instead, and rejoins for
  the on-lead work.
  "They have to see me in a behaviour consultation first, so I can assess
  whether it's suitable for them to be in class at all. If the aggression is
  controllable, they can come to class, but they are not let off the lead
  to play with other dogs. They can only come out, stand outside the class
  and watch them run around. And then when we got them all back on the
  lead and we work with them, they come back in on a lead." [seg 100]

- **Friendly but over-excited dogs (bark/lunge to reach other dogs, not
  aggressive): welcome.** Run off lead first to burn energy, then worked on
  focus at their own level.
  "I always have overexcited dogs in class. We run them off lead first so
  they run off a bit of energy, and then get them to concentrate on their
  own level." [seg 108]

- **Neutering and seasons.** Dogs don't need to be neutered or spayed. A
  bitch in season must miss three weeks; she's moved to the next course and
  credited for the classes she didn't use.
  "If a dog comes into heat mid-season, they cannot come to class for three
  weeks anyway. So I move them on to the next course... and I will credit
  them for any classes they never use." [seg 147–148]

- **XL Bullies, and any dog legally required to be muzzled and kept on lead
  in public, are not accepted** — Gaynor's insurance does not cover them,
  and they would not be run free regardless.
  "I wouldn't have them running free." [seg 197] "XL Bullies. I don't have
  XL Bullies in class. So the insurance won't cover them anymore." [seg 199]

- **Bronze is open at any age above the site's stated minimum — a dog is
  never too old to start.** A dog that has shown aggression, or is
  extremely difficult to control (e.g. dragging the owner over), needs a
  behaviour consultation first.
  "The never too old to come to bronze course... no matter what age, they
  can come. But if they have shown aggression or if they are extremely
  difficult, dragging a person, pulling them over... then you would come
  and do a behavior consultation first." [seg 215] — see the editorial note
  on the age figure in the transcript, and Open Questions below.

- **Junior and Bronze age boundaries flex around a conversation.** The site
  states Junior as roughly 6–12 months and Bronze as 12 months and over;
  Gaynor will move a dog earlier or later than those bands after talking it
  through.
  "It depends on what they want. I'd recommend they go junior bronze. If
  they particularly want to go bronze, I'll put them in bronze." [seg 138]
  (context: Courtenay's framing questions at [seg 132, 136] put the
  boundaries at six and twelve months; Gaynor did not dispute either
  number.)
  **Replaces:** the site's former Junior-course wording "between 5 and 9
  months," which contradicted every other course page and this interview.

- **Skipping levels** (going straight to Silver, Gold or Platinum without
  the earlier courses) is judged by a conversation about what the dog can
  already do, not a formal assessment.
  "Speak to them... meet up with [them], ask them what they can do with
  their dog and then I'll know them." [seg 217–219]
  **Amended 2026-09-18** (Gaynor's written feedback on the FAQ page, then her
  WhatsApp reply to a follow-up, both relayed by Courtenay): the answer to
  "can we go straight to Silver / Gold / Platinum?" is **yes, if at the
  right obedience level** — Platinum included.
  "Anyone can join any of the groups provided their dog is at the right
  level." "Platinum course dates are dictated by having enough people
  wanting to go to the course, it just depends on the demand for the
  course." (WhatsApp, 2026-09-18)
  The Silver, Gold and Platinum "Who it's for" lines now read "<previous
  level> graduates, or dogs already at that level"; Platinum's start note
  says it runs on demand rather than being agreed at Gold graduation.

## Puppy Socialisation

- **The vet decides when a puppy can start** — not a fixed number of
  vaccinations or a fixed wait. Book with Gaynor once the vet has cleared
  it.
  "They should ask the vet when their dog puppy is ready to go to classes
  and then come to me to book a place. As the vet dictates." [seg 10–11]
  "Some vets use one vaccination and other vets use a different vaccination.
  Some vets require one week after the vaccination for the dog to come in,
  and other vets require two weeks before. So it always goes back to the
  vet." [seg 48]
  **Replaces:** the site's former FAQ statement that "a puppy needs all its
  vaccinations before it can attend."

- **No deposit for Puppy Socialisation.** Book a place and pay when you
  come.
  "They don't pay a deposit, puppy... they just book a place and they come
  and they pay." [seg 13–15]

- **Always Sunday at 3:00pm.**
  "It's always on a Sunday at three o'clock." [seg 21]

- **No proof of vaccination needed in advance** — bring the vet card to the
  first class, not before.
  "Not before. They're going to bring it to their first class." [seg 50]

- **No kennel cough vaccination needed to attend.**
  "We don't need kennel cough to come to class." [seg 52]

- **Practise at home before the first class:** getting used to a collar and
  a lead (out to the garden), responding to their name, and making eye
  contact — treats help with the last two.
  "Putting the dog on a lead and taking it out to the gardens. We get used
  to a lead." [seg 24] "Calling him by name, so he gets used to his name."
  [seg 26] "Using treats, getting the dog to look at them and making eye
  contact with the dog." [seg 29]

- **Toilet training, crate training and chewing are covered in class** —
  ask anything you want.
  "It's covered in class." [seg 36] "You can ask any question you want in
  class." [seg 38]

- **A shy or late-socialised puppy:** if still young, come to class as
  normal; from about six months, ring first so Gaynor can advise on that
  individual dog.
  "If the dog is still young, we come to puppy classes. But if it's a
  little bit older, six months, you need to ring me and speak to me and
  tell me how the dog is like, so I can make a better decision." [seg 40]

- **Turning six months partway through the course:** keep going — the dog
  doesn't have to leave.
  "It's fine. It doesn't matter if they had six months in the middle of a
  course. Just keep on going." [seg 44]

- **Joining a course already underway:** only in the second week, and only
  in exceptional circumstances cleared with Gaynor first; otherwise wait
  for the next course.
  "If I got it wrong, they can join on the second week. But if it goes to
  the third, they may as well just wait for the next course." [seg 33]
  "Only in exceptional circumstances and only if they cleared it with you
  first. That's right." [seg 55–56]

## One-to-one and behaviour consultations

- **£90 for a one-hour session at A1K9; £130 for a home visit, plus travel
  costs if any distance away.** The price is the same whether it's called a
  one-to-one or a behavioural consultation.
  "It's ninety pound for the hour session. That's held at A1K9." [seg 230]
  "It's ninety pound if you come to me. It's a hundred and thirty if I go
  to you. And if you're any distance away from me, it does incur travelling
  costs as well." [seg 235] "Whether it's a one-to-one or whether it's a
  behaviour consultation, it's the same." [seg 241]
  **Replaces:** the site's former line, "One-to-one and behavioural
  consultations are arranged to suit you… prices when you get in touch."

- **Aggression consultations are always at A1K9, never at home** (added
  2026-09-18, Gaynor's WhatsApp feedback on the consultation pages, relayed
  by Courtenay). Applies to both dog-on-dog and dog-on-person aggression;
  the £130 home visit remains for other behavioural work.
  "Any dog aggression consultation must be done at A1K9 training grounds.
  (too dangerous at home)"

- **Booking a session: form, reply, phone call** (2026-09-18, same
  source). Step 1, send the form (unchanged). "Step two. Gaynor will reply
  to your email giving you all the information you need. Step 3. Phone
  Gaynor to have a chat about the problem you are having with your dog and
  make an appointment if deemed necessary."

- **"What to expect" ends at the equipment demonstration** (2026-09-18,
  same source). The site no longer says Gaynor may put you on a
  rehabilitation programme or monitor you by email/phone, nor that she
  writes a full report for your vet after an ordinary consultation. (The

- **Dog-on-person consultations test with A1K9 staff, not A1K9 dogs**
  (2026-09-18, same source). "She will test your dog for aggressive
  behaviour towards people using a1k9 staff if necessary."

- **Aggression experience: over 20 years** (2026-09-18, same source),
  replacing "15 years" on the aggression pages. Given for the dog-on-person
  page; applied to the identical sentence on the dog-on-dog page and the
  About page's pack-experience line too. No longer typed: the pages use
  `{{experienceYears}}`, computed at build time from
  `business.experienceSince` = 2006 ("I was dog training in 2006", unsure
  how much earlier, so it is a floor) and rounded down to a multiple of 5.
  It reads "over 25 years" from 2031 without anyone editing it.

- **Usually seen within about a week of enquiring.**
  "I normally work one week ahead." [seg 251]

- **One session is normally enough — no packages of sessions.**
  "I don't normally see people more than once because once I've seen them
  and explained everything and worked with them, they normally know enough
  then to carry on their own or to come into a course to continue." [seg
  232–233]

- **Two dogs from the same home:** seen together in one session, at no
  extra charge, with no separate booking. Where the two dogs fight each
  other, both must attend.
  "Both dogs come. Sometimes it takes a little bit longer. That's okay."
  [seg 142] (no extra charge, no separate booking: [seg 143–144])
  "Both dogs must come." [seg 237]

- **A one-to-one can be purely to assess a dog and recommend a course.**
  "If they're having problems and they're finding it difficult, I'll do a
  one to one with them. I'll get them up and running and then I'll assess
  whether they are suitable to come in to a course." [seg 228]

- **Preparing a dog for a new baby:** yes — book about two months ahead.
  "I've done that. You could do that." [seg 254] "A couple of months. Two
  months." [seg 258–259]

- **Separation anxiety, noise/firework fear and car anxiety are all
  behavioural work.** Ring for a chat about what's happening.
  "It's a good idea to ring me and have a little chat about exactly what's
  happened." [seg 261] "They are behavioural problems. They are very much
  so." [seg 263]

- **Staying safe between enquiring and the consultation:** don't confront
  the dog (e.g. forcing an item out of its mouth) — wait to be shown how to
  handle it, and get in touch as soon as possible.
  "Don't have a confrontation with the dog. That's most important... Get in
  touch as soon as possible and I will see them as soon as possible." [seg
  245] "The dog's picked something up and he's running around with it. Then
  you force the dog, give me that, and the dog goes under the table. You go
  under the table and try to take it off. It's going to bite you. Just
  don't do it." [seg 248]

  "It's really not suitable for anyone that's disabled. You cannot get a
  wheelchair. We're walking in fields. You've got to climb up a hill, which
  is rough ground. It's just not suitable for people." [seg 165] "Have I
  got toilets there? Yes... in both locations." [seg 167–168]
  **Replaces:** no prior site statement — this is a new, honest access note
  the operator directed be published on the Contact and Facilities pages.

- **The indoor/office space is level and suitable, but Gaynor mostly works
  outdoors**, so a visit is only partly accessible even where the space

  166]

## What Gaynor does not offer

- **No standalone agility, gundog training, CaniCross or protection work.**
  Agility only appears inside the Silver/Gold curriculum (sometimes
  Bronze), for dogs over twelve months.
  "I don't do any of those. But I do agility as part of the course in
  silver and gold, sometimes in bronze, because they can't do agility until
  they're over twelve months old... it's part of the obedience course. I
  don't do standalone classes." [seg 115]

- **Protection work, and the five-week residential obedience course, are
  run by A1K9 (Charles) — not by Gaynor.**
  "The five-week residential course is with A1K9." [seg 189] "He doesn't do
  daily training. It's just a five week one he does." [seg 191]
  "Obedience five week obedience training course." [seg 195]

- **No specific PAT/therapy-dog training offered**, though the obedience
  training helps towards it.
  "It certainly helps because you're teaching them to be calmer, how to
  meet people in a controlled way, how to be a much more controlled dog...
  but it's not something that I do per se, particularly for PAT dogs." [seg
  163]

## Approach and awards

- **Approach:** touch training and treat-oriented at first, moving on to
  working without touching the lead, using body language and voice
  commands.
  "My approach is touch training and treat orientated, then going on to not
  touching the lead and doing body language and voice command." [seg 171]

- **No shop, but Gaynor sells high quality leads and collars** (2026-09-18,
  WhatsApp feedback on the About pages, relayed by Courtenay). "Take off
  shop. But it would be helpful to mention somewhere on the site that I sell
  high quality leads and collars. I can supply photos." The Facilities
  "Shop" card is gone; `business.courses.equipmentNote` puts the line under
  "Bring" on every course page. Photos pending.

- **No clicker training** (2026-09-18, Gaynor's WhatsApp feedback on the
  Courses page and her yes to a follow-up, relayed by Courtenay). Removed
  from the Courses page puppy card, the Puppy Socialisation page and the
  Junior page ("touch training" stays); don't reintroduce it.

- **Prong collars:** not something Gaynor has encountered — no site
  statement is needed either way.
  "I've never heard of a prong collar before." [seg 170]

- **Awards.** Each week teaches a new skill; week six reviews everything
  covered. Get it right and you get that level's rosette (e.g. the Bronze
  rosette). Not quite there yet and you get an "achievement" rosette
  instead — you can still move up, and earn the level rosette in a later
  course once you've got it.
  "Every week I teach them something different. Then I go over everything
  I've done on the sixth week... if they do it correctly, they will have a
  rosette. If they haven't quite got it, they will have a rosette but they
  won't have the junior rosette." [seg 223] "It doesn't mean they can't
  move up; they can move up, and when they reach the right level and
  they've got it, I will give them the rosette in the following course."
  [seg 224] "I call the other one an achievement... it's an achievement
  award because they have achieved so much." [seg 226]

## Open questions

None outstanding. The five the interview left ambiguous were resolved on
2026-09-15 by Courtenay Probert, who conducted the interview, understood
Gaynor's answers on the call and is confident in these. They are
confirmed; quote them as policy, not as Gaynor's verbatim words:

- **Bronze minimum age — 12 months and over.** Segment 215's "anything
  over two and a half months" is a mis-hearing of "twelve months"; the
  site's Bronze minimum stands.
- **Platinum's day — no fixed day.** Deliberately unstated: a course runs
  when there is enough demand (Gaynor, 2026-09-18; this replaces "agreed
  with each group at Gold graduation"). The At a glance block
  shows no "When" row for Platinum; do not restore "Saturday afternoons".
- **The £50 deposit — confirmed.** Non-refundable, by bank transfer, none
  for Puppy Socialisation.
- **Catchment — towns only, no radius, by design.** The towns Gaynor named
  are examples of where her classes draw from; the point she was making is
  that the owner evaluates the drive and decides for themselves. Do not
  add a distance or time; keep the contact page's "it comes down to how
  far you're happy to travel".
- **Wet-weather venue switch — confirmed.** Outdoors at the Training
  Grounds; indoors at Llys Nini Animal Centre when it is wet, as
  `src/config/business.js` `venueNote` says.
